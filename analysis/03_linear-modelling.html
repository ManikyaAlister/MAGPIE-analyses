Before/After Change variables

Bayesian linear modelling for the variables that were measured both before and after the experiment. The general structure of the models was: Variable After ~ Variable Before + Condition, thus essentially measuring the change in each variable by condition.

Predicting changes in measures as a function of the types of posts seen

We were also able to collect data on the exact posts that people saw. This can provide insight into what it was that affected participants’ experiences and was shifting people’s beliefs and perceptions of consensus in the troll conditions.

Comparing sample characteristics for full sample versus sample with posts seen data available

Because posts-seen data could not be collected for every participant, we checked that the sub-sample with posts-seen data did not differ from the full sample. This comparison is reported in the Supplementary Materials (04_supplementary-materials.qmd).

What impact does seeing troll-initated content have on our measures? And how does this compare to condition effects?

We tested whether changes in each measure could be predicted by the type of content people saw: the proportion of friendly content, right-wing content or left-wing content.

We then repeated this analysis excluding content from troll-initiated conversations (defined as troll posts and any participant replies to those posts), to test whether the overall shift in discourse predicted outcomes above and beyond direct troll influence. Because troll posts were absent from the control condition by design, this analysis was restricted to participants in the and conditions. Troll-initiated content accounted for approximately 16% of posts seen by users in these conditions.

The Figure below shows the posterior coefficients for each content-based predictor (Right'',Left”, ``Friendly’’) across all outcome measures, comparing models fitted on all content to models fitted on non-troll-initiated content only. Each model also included the corresponding before-measurement as a covariate, so coefficients reflect change in each measure rather than absolute levels. Points that are transparent indicate credible intervals that overlap zero. This analysis was not conducted for relative belief or affective polarisation measures, as these showed no credible condition effects.
